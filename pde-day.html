Titles & Abstracts

Invited Talks
Antoine MelletUniversity of Maryland · 11:00
Cell sorting by differential adhesion: a sharp-interface limit of Keller–Segel systems

In the 1960s, M. Steinberg proposed that mixed populations of cells sort themselves the way immiscible fluids do: cells of different types adhere with different strengths, and the tissue rearranges to minimize an interfacial energy. This Differential Adhesion Hypothesis predicts several possible equilibrium configurations (mixing, complete sorting, partial or full engulfment of one cell type by the other), depending only on the relative adhesion coefficients. In this talk, I will explain how this picture can be derived from a Keller–Segel system describing several species that interact through short-range attraction of different strengths and a common nonlinear pressure. We show that the densities segregate and that the energy Γ-converges to a multiphase perimeter functional. The surface tension coefficients are given by geodesic distances between the wells of an effective potential for an appropriate distance, and the equality cases in the corresponding triangle inequalities identify the regimes predicted by Steinberg. Finally, under a standard energy convergence assumption, we show that the dynamics converges to a multiphase Hele–Shaw flow with contact angle conditions at triple junctions. This is joint work with Jiwoong Jang.

Huy NguyenUniversity of Maryland · 12:00
Suppression of the Benjamin–Feir instability by constant vorticity

We study the effect of constant vorticity on the Benjamin–Feir instability of small-amplitude Stokes waves in infinite depth. Working with the full water-wave equations, we rigorously establish a transition between modulational instability and stability as the vorticity crosses a critical value. We consider right-propagating waves with constant vorticity γ, associated with the background shear current −γy ex, and normalize the gravitational acceleration and carrier wavenumber to one. For each fixed γ < 2/√3, we prove that sufficiently small-amplitude Stokes waves are Benjamin–Feir unstable. For each fixed γ > 2/√3, these waves are spectrally stable with respect to longitudinal, long-wavelength perturbations. These results rigorously confirm, away from the critical value, the stability transition predicted by the formal nonlinear Schrödinger approximation of Thomas, Kharif, and Manna. Based on joint work with Bowen Chen (UMD).

Deniz BilmanUniversity of Cincinnati · 1:30
Beyond soliton resolution: anomalous temporal decay

The cubic focusing nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equation is a universal model for the evolution of weakly nonlinear, dispersive wave packets, arising in diverse physical settings ranging from deep-water waves to nonlinear optics. For generic, sufficiently localized initial data, its long-time dynamics are described by soliton resolution: a finite collection of solitons accompanied by dispersive radiation decaying at the rate t−1/2. This is a particular instance of the broader soliton resolution conjecture for Hamiltonian dispersive PDEs.

Curiously, in recent joint work, we showed that rogue waves of infinite order, a family of solutions of the focusing NLS equation, behave strikingly differently: although they generate no solitons or other persistent structures, they exhibit anomalously slow temporal decay at the rate t−1/3 and spread slowly as they evolve. These solutions have finite L2-mass but are not spatially integrable, and therefore fall outside the standard framework of inverse-scattering theory. They also emerge as universal profiles in several distinct asymptotic regimes involving amplitude explosion, including large-order limits of rogue waves and solitons and the dispersive regularization of catastrophic self-focusing. In this talk, I will review these results and discuss ongoing work aimed at understanding the mechanism responsible for such slow decay, particularly through the lens of scattering theory and its limitations for slowly decaying initial data.

Guher CamliyurtVirginia Tech · 2:30
Scattering results for radial solutions of focusing supercritical wave equations in even dimensions

Conditional well-posedness and scattering results for bounded solutions of nonlinear dispersive equations have been studied extensively. In particular, the program based on the concentration-compactness and rigidity methods, initiated by Kenig and Merle in 2008, has generated a vast body of literature. In this talk, we will review the applications of those methods for focusing energy-supercritical nonlinear wave equations and discuss the unique challenges posed by analogous problems in even dimensions. One of the main goals is to review the changes to the essential steps of rigidity methods in dimension four versus higher even dimensions.

Sarswati ShahGeorge Mason University · 4:00
QH-GEM: Quantum-hydrodynamic generative modeling

Generative models can be interpreted as transporting a simple reference probability distribution toward a target distribution. In many modern approaches, the transport map, velocity field, or score function is learned from data. This talk presents a different perspective in which the transport mechanism is prescribed by a physical PDE. We introduce QH-GEM, a deterministic, physics-constrained generative framework based on the Madelung formulation of the free-particle Schrödinger equation. A reference probability density evolved together with a controllable initial phase, whose gradient determines the hydrodynamic velocity field and hence the characteristic flow that transports samples. Once the initial phase is fixed, randomness enters only through the initial sampling; the subsequent evolution is deterministic. This viewpoint leads naturally to a PDE-constrained phase-identification problem for matching a prescribed target distribution. The talk discusses the underlying transport structure, exact reachability results for Gaussian distributions, a short-time connection with classical transport maps, and numerical examples demonstrating phase identification for non-Gaussian targets. These results provide a mathematical proof of concept for phase-controlled quantum hydrodynamics as a deterministic framework for generative modeling.

Christopher IrvingGeorgetown University · 4:30
Korn-type inequalities for general differential constraints

I will discuss the validity of Korn-type inequalities for general constant-coefficient differential operators satisfying a constant rank condition. Such inequalities are motivated by applications to certain variational problems, where endpoint variants played an essential role in establishing the regularity of minimizers. I will present a local inequality and discuss the challenges in obtaining global estimates valid up to the boundary. This is joint work with Zhuolin Li (Bonn) and Bogdan Raiţă (Georgetown).

Ryan PattersonUniversity of Maryland · 3:00
Convergence to traveling waves for an FKPP model with nonlinear advection

We establish and quantify the convergence of solutions to traveling waves for a reaction-diffusion model with nonlinear advection. The convergence rates are proven primarily through analyzing the shape defect function. We introduce a novel energy methods-based approach that uses a weighted Nash inequality to obtain L∞-estimates on the shape defect function. In determining the convergence rates, we also establish the front location for the model.

Jun ParkUniversity of Virginia · 3:10
Temperature noise forces convection in the Rayleigh–Bénard system

Rayleigh–Bénard convection is the flow of a fluid layer heated from below, driven by buoyancy: hot fluid rises and cold fluid sinks. The vertical heat transport is measured by the Nusselt number Nu, which equals 1 when conduction carries all the heat and exceeds 1 when convection contributes. For the deterministic equations, no nontrivial lower bound on Nu can hold: the purely conductive state is a solution with Nu = 1 at every Rayleigh number. We ask whether random forcing in the temperature alone changes this, for the two-dimensional system with additive noise. To answer this, we review the auxiliary functional method, a systematic way to bound long-time averages, and extend it to stochastic PDEs. This gives lower bounds on the expected time-averaged Nusselt number that are explicit in the Rayleigh number, the Prandtl number and the noise.

Jianlei HuangGeorgetown University · 5:00
Well-posedness for the cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation with nonlinearity supported on a hypersurface

The Schrödinger equation has been studied for nearly a century as one of the foundational equations in quantum mechanics, and its behavior under various potentials has been extensively explored. In this talk, we will present our recent results concerning the well-posedness of the nonlinear Schrödinger equation with nonlinearity concentrated on a hypersurface. We prove that the 2-d cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation with nonlinearity supported on a line or a sphere is globally well-posed in H1(ℝ2). For higher dimensions, we prove that the equation with hyperplane-supported nonlinearity is locally well-posed in suitable anisotropic Sobolev spaces. We also prove that the equation with sphere-supported nonlinearity is globally well-posed in H1(ℝn) with radial data.

Austin HoUniversity of Virginia · 5:10
On a classification of GOY/Sabra-type shell models of turbulence

Since their inception in the 1970s, shell models have proved to be a fruitful testbed for studying theories of turbulence. This talk considers variations of two classical shell models: the Gledzer–Ohkitani–Yamada (GOY) model and the Sabra model. We will mainly address the numerical approximation of the scaling exponents for the newly identified models. We also briefly touch on their mathematical well-posedness and conjugacy classification of all possible GOY-type and Sabra-type models. This work is based on my master's thesis, advised by Professor Vincent R. Martinez (CUNY Hunter College & Graduate Center), with Professor Aseel Farhat (University of Virginia).

Kunhui LuanUniversity of South Carolina · 5:20
Uniform-in-diffusivity mixing by shear flows: stochastic and dynamical perspectives

We study the mixing of passive scalars by parallel shear flows in the presence of weak molecular diffusion. In particular, we recover the sharp uniform-in-diffusivity mixing rates for shear flows with finitely many critical points, a result recently established by Albritton and Beekie. Our approach is based on the stochastic representation formula of the associated advection-diffusion equation. This probabilistic framework yields two short proofs.

The first generalizes the estimate of oscillatory integrals from the zero-diffusivity case and establishes the optimal mixing rate under the weakest possible regularity assumption, answering the question regarding the sharp regularity posed in Section 4 of Albritton & Beekie.

The second adopts a dynamical systems perspective and provides a proof of shear flow mixing in a different topology, which, to our knowledge, is new even in the zero-diffusivity setting. This is joint work with Kyle L. Liss.
